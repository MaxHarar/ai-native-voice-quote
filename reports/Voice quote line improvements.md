# Fix short answers, then measure everything

**The five highest-value additions, in rank order, are: (1) retune turn-taking for one-word and identifier answers, (2) add an audio-layer eval tier plus per-call production metrics, (3) harden consent and election handling, (4) add VIN decode and garaging-address standardization, and (5) add a post-call follow-up and fallback-callback path.** The first targets the exact failure seen in your 13 real calls (dropped one-word replies) and costs a config change plus a test. The second is what proves the first worked: text-only evals badly overstate voice performance, so your pass^2 text suite cannot see this class of failure. The third is cheap and removes legal exposure that grows with every call. The fourth attacks identifier accuracy, where general ASR is the weakest. The fifth has the thinnest evidence and the longest lead time, because it is gated by messaging registration. Evidence quality is uneven throughout: most sources are vendor documentation and blogs, there is no independent benchmark of modern ASR on 8 kHz spelled identifiers, and no independent AI-voice quote-to-bind data exists. Where a recommendation rests on vendor claims, that is stated. Compliance findings are research, not legal advice, and several rest on secondary sources.

## Your dropped one-word answers are a configuration problem you can test this week

ElevenLabs documents `turn_eagerness: patient` as the setting for "collecting phone numbers, addresses, or email addresses," and its recommended table for information collection pairs a 10 to 15 second timeout with patient eagerness ([ElevenLabs conversation flow](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/eleven-agents/customization/conversation-flow.md)). You already match the timeout (10 s) but run `normal` eagerness. Your observed failure, one-word replies that never finalize, fits the documented mechanism: the turn closes or the ASR commits before the word lands. This is an inference. No source measures how often short answers drop under each setting, and nothing documents turn_v3 internals, so the fix is a hypothesis to test, not a proven cure. The tradeoff is a few hundred milliseconds of added wait on exactly the slots where accuracy matters more than speed. A related platform, Vapi, hard-codes the same insight by waiting 0.5 s after a transcript ending in a number versus 0.1 s after punctuation ([Vapi docs](https://docs.vapi.ai/customization/voice-pipeline-configuration)), and LiveKit's forum documents turns flushing early when the final transcript arrives late ([LiveKit community](https://community.livekit.io/t/cloud-turn-detector-failed-late-stt-final-warnings-after-enabling-livekit-inference-audio-turn-detection-vad/1472)).

Build three things. First, set patient eagerness for yes/no confirmations, digit strings and spell-backs, keeping normal elsewhere; if the platform cannot vary eagerness per slot, test patient globally and measure the latency cost. Second, enable the soft timeout (documented range 0.5 to 8 s, recommended 3.0 s, fires once per turn only when the LLM or a tool is slow) to cover rating and MVR/CLUE lookups, avoiding "one second" style filler per ElevenLabs guidance. Third, add a prompt rule that any transcript that looks like a fragment or noise triggers a re-ask rather than acceptance. Also disable interruptions during the recording disclosure and quote read-out, which ElevenLabs lists as the configuration for legal disclaimers.

Your proposed noise plan should be trimmed. DTMF fallback is the weakest item: ElevenLabs' documented keypad tool is outbound only, and one translated doc page suggests the out-of-band DTMF option is ignored for Twilio native calls, so inbound keypad capture on your stack is unconfirmed ([ElevenLabs keypad tool](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/eleven-agents/customization/tools/system-tools/play-keypad-touch-tone)). Vendor glossaries also warn that a keypress the agent never reacts to makes callers hang up ([Dograh](https://www.dograh.com/feeds/blog/dtmf-detection-voice-agent)). Run one live test of a single-key control before committing. The research notes contain no evidence on `skip_turn` or silence auto-hangup, so treat those as unresearched, not rejected. Your calls showed quiet failures, not loudness, which also makes noise suppression a low-yield target.

## Text evals overstate voice quality, so the measurement layer comes second

The strongest independent evidence in the whole research set is tau-Voice, which extends the tau2-bench family to full-duplex voice: GPT-5 reasoning scores **80% on text** but voice agents reach only **29 to 42% in clean conditions and 19 to 30% with noise and diverse accents**, reported as pass@1 ([arXiv 2603.13686](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.13686)). Your 13 tau-style evals at pass^2 1.00 test the LLM and tools, not the audio path where your real failures occurred. Hamming claims text-only testing misses about 40% of voice-specific failures, but that number is a vendor claim relayed by a competitor ([Future AGI comparison](https://futureagi.com/blogs/voice-ai-simulation-cekura-hamming-bluejay-coval-2025)).

The cheapest route uses what you have. ElevenLabs Tests supports simulated conversations with deterministic checks on tool parameters, tests generated from past conversations, and CI use ([ElevenLabs blog](https://elevenlabs.io/blog/tests-for-elevenlabs-agents)). Because your rating engine is deterministic, assert on tool-call arguments (state, DOB, vehicle, coverage election) and final numbers, and reserve an LLM judge for tone, disclosure phrasing and hand-off wording. Convert each of the 13 real calls that failed into a regression task, add perturbation variants (one-word answers, mid-flow corrections, barge-in, noisy name and VIN captures), and raise k to 5 on the critical flows. The open-source tau2-bench repo now includes end-to-end voice evaluation with realtime providers if you want an external reference ([sierra-research/tau2-bench](https://github.com/sierra-research/tau2-bench)). Paid simulators (Hamming, Coval, Cekura, Bluejay) are unnecessary at demo scale, and no independent head-to-head comparison or pricing was found.

For production, replace the manual baseline script with per-call metrics logged to SQLite: time to first word and tool latency percentiles, re-ask count per slot (DOB, license, VIN, address, name spell-back), fragment-rejection count, transfer reasons, and a redefined "containment" meaning quote completed, application number issued, or intentional hand-off. Alert on rolling percentiles, not averages; Hamming notes a 500 ms average can hide 10% of calls above 3 s ([Hamming](https://hamming.ai/blog/how-to-evaluate-voice-agents-2026)). Hamming's thresholds (task completion above 85%, retry rate under 5%, abandonment under 8%) are a usable starting scorecard, but they are vendor-sourced and internally inconsistent ([Hamming KPI guide](https://hamming.ai/blog/voice-agent-monitoring-kpis-production-guide)). A nightly LLM pass that tags each transcript with a failure mode (mishear, loop, wrong tool args, compliance slip, premature hand-off) closes the loop; no source quantifies tagging accuracy, so spot-check it by hand. Effort is M, mostly the perturbation fixtures and the metrics table.

## Consent and election handling carry real risk, and some items are blockers

Treat the line as all-party-consent in every state. Pennsylvania requires consent of all participants to record ([RCFP PA guide](https://www.rcfp.org/reporters-recording-guide/pennsylvania/)), and courts find consent where parties knew or should have known of recording, citing Commonwealth v. Byrd (Pa. 2020). New Jersey is one-party ([RCFP NJ guide](https://www.rcfp.org/reporters-recording-guide/new-jersey/)), though all-party bills reportedly sit in committee (status unconfirmed). Michigan's statute requires permission of "all persons engaged" ([DMLP MI](https://www.dmlp.org/node/1297)), while appellate cases treat a participant as not an eavesdropper and the question is unsettled ([Barnes & Thornburg](https://uatupgrade.btlaw.com/insights/alerts/2020/michigan-supreme-court-to-clarify-interpretation-of-michigans-eavesdropping-statute)). Your opening already discloses the AI, the recording and non-binding quotes, which covers the baseline. The extension is a real decline path ("if you'd rather not be recorded, say so now and I'll connect you with a licensed specialist"), a stored consent record (disclosure timestamp, call ID, continued participation), and a restatement of recording status when the conference transfer reaches the human. These are inferences from the legal sources, not statutory script requirements; counsel should confirm whether continued participation suffices in PA and whether vendor-side recording by ElevenLabs and Twilio is a separate interception.

The second item is PA tort handling. Section 1705 requires a standardized notice and an affirmative election for limited tort, with full tort as default, and signing an application did not cure a missing statutory form in Hockenberry ([PA statute](https://www.legis.state.pa.us/WU01/LI/LI/CT/HTM/75/00.017.005.000..HTM); [TortTalk](https://www.torttalk.com/2012/03/pennsylvania-superior-court-addresses.html)). A verbal election is therefore not final. Have the agent quote both options, state that the binding election happens on the signed form through the licensed specialist, and default to full tort until then. Your Michigan PIP flow with server-owned disclosure text already follows the right pattern, since an effective opt-out needs all statutory conditions and a DIFS-approved form ([Michigan Auto Law](https://www.michiganautolaw.com/no-fault/no-fault-act/pip-medical-benefits-coverage/)). Say "simulated reports" out loud wherever MVR, CLUE or prior-insurance data appears.

Three items are blockers, not improvements, for any real consumer use. Selling, soliciting or negotiating insurance without a producer license is prohibited in all three states, and "solicit" includes urging someone to apply for a particular insurer's product ([FindLaw 40 P.S. 310.1](https://codes.findlaw.com/pa/title-40-ps-insurance/pa-st-sect-40-310-1/); [MCL 500.1201](https://legislature.mi.gov/documents/mcl/pdf/MCL-500-1201.pdf)). Your hand-off-to-specialist design is the right shape, so add guardrails (never recommend limits, never say "you should buy") and operate under a licensed agency before real use. If reports ever go live, FCRA adverse-action duties attach to the user of the report. And NJ basic versus standard policy disclosure was not found in the research; verify it before NJ launch. Finally, NJ, PA and MI all adopted the NAIC AI bulletin ([Locke Lord](https://lockelord.com/newsandevents/publications/2024/08/michigan-issued-bulletin-202420ins-re-use-of-ais-i); [Quarles](https://quarles.com/newsroom/publications/nearly-half-of-states-have-now-adopted-naic-model-bulletin-on-insurers-use-of-ai)), which binds insurers, not a demo operator, but a carrier partner will ask for artifacts. Keep an AI system inventory, prompt change log, eval results (item 2 produces these), incident log and vendor list. No NJ, PA or MI chatbot-disclosure statute turned up, which is a search gap, not proof of absence. Effort is S plus counsel time.

## Identifier accuracy is where voice quoting leaks, and two free tools plug it

A Sheffield corpus reported through Speechmatics showed **96.6% word accuracy but only 77% exact-match identifiers**, rising to 91.7% with identifier-specific adaptation ([Speechmatics](https://speechmatics.com/company/articles-and-news/alphanumeric-speech-recognition-why-voice-assistants-mangle-skus-and-how-to-fix-it)). That is a different domain and vendor-reported, so read it as directional, but it matches your license-digit re-asks. The lever the sources favor is constraint plus validation plus read-back, which you partly have. A VIN is the worst slot to take by voice: 17 characters with letter and digit confusion. NHTSA's vPIC decoder is free and keyless, reports about 99% decode reliability for model year 1995 and later, and handles roughly 1,000 to 2,000 transactions per minute, though the FAQ and About page conflict on rate limits and the batch cap is unstated ([NHTSA vPIC FAQ](https://vpic.nhtsa.dot.gov/api/home/index/faq)). Build a flow where the caller gives year, make and model, optionally reads the VIN, the server validates the check digit, decodes through vPIC, and the agent confirms the decoded vehicle ("a 2021 Honda Civic, correct?"). Cache results and fall back to your simulated data on timeout, since one anecdotal report puts latency above 2.5 s. The check digit is a free ASR-error catch regardless of vPIC. vPIC returns attributes, not ISO rating symbols; those need a commercial source the research could not verify.

Address standardization is the second half. Insurers price partly on garaging location, so ask where the car is kept overnight and standardize that address, not the billing one ([Precisely case study](https://www.precisely.com/resource-center/customer-story/us-insurer-automates-vehicle-territorial-ratings-for-regulation-compliance/)). Smarty offers a free 42-day, 1,000-lookup trial, enough for the demo, with conflicting paid pricing across sources ([Smarty](https://www.smarty.com/pricing/us-address-verification)). Flag ZIPs that span municipalities, since an NJ bill would shift exposures to municipal boundaries ([LegiScan S270](https://legiscan.com/NJ/text/S270/2026)). Skip commercial prefill: LexisNexis, Verisk and TransUnion products are enterprise-only and require FCRA permissible purpose, and their "20 minutes to under 5" figure is a vendor claim ([LexisNexis](https://risk.lexisnexis.com/-/media/files/insurance/brochure/auto_data_prefill%20pdf.pdf)). Keep simulation labeled. Telematics offers are lower priority, with no conversion evidence. Effort is S for the check digit and vPIC, M with address work.

## Follow-up is worth building, but its lift evidence is the weakest and its gate is the longest

The best independent conversion number is old: J.D. Power 2017 found 33% of agent quotes closed versus 10% of website quotes, and online chat users were 89% more likely to close ([Dig-In](https://www.dig-in.com/news/insurers-websites-struggle-to-close-deals-jd-power)). The durable lesson is that human involvement and speed matter, which your hand-off already uses. Speed-to-lead data is directional: a 2020 mystery shop of 150 agencies found only 6% responded within 5 minutes and 2% used text ([HawkSoft](https://blog.hawksoft.com/speed-2-lead-case-study)), but it measured response time, not conversion. Vendor SMS claims (25 to 40% reply, 15 to 25% conversion) and bolt's "doubles call-to-bind" are self-reported with no baseline ([EZ Texting](https://www.eztexting.com/use-cases/insurance/multi-service); [bolt release](https://markets.financialcontent.com/worldnow.ktul/article/accwirecq-2025-10-15-bolt-launches-ai-for-agencies-to-reduce-quoting-time-and-improve-bind-rates)). Plan against 25 to 35% quote-to-bind as a rough ceiling, not a benchmark.

Build the recap around three parts. The SMS carries brand, "AI-generated quote, not a binding offer," a short tokenized expiring link to a secure resume page, and "Reply STOP to opt out." It contains no DOB, license, VIN, full address or name-plus-premium, because SMS is unencrypted. The specialist hand-off passes a context packet (name, state, vehicles and drivers, chosen option, price, DOB-verified flag) so the human does not re-ask, with a timed fallback callback if the transfer rings out; transfers sent to voicemail are reportedly lost, though that source is vendor-only ([Sonant](https://www.sonant.ai/blog/auto-insurance-live-transfer-leads)). Your DOB-verified resume codes already match the resume-link pattern.

The gates are real. A2P 10DLC registration is required, and Twilio's sole-proprietor tier allows one campaign, one number and one message per second, with EIN holders routed to a different tier; those Twilio pages are dated ([Twilio limitations](https://support.twilio.com/hc/en-us/articles/1260804728069-Current-Limitations-of-A2P-10DLC)). Capture spoken consent on the call ("Can I text a summary to the number you're calling from?"), store timestamp, number and call ID, honor any reasonable opt-out phrase (the FCC reasonable-means rule, 10 business days at most, per law-firm summaries: [Kelley Drye](https://www.kelleydrye.com/viewpoints/blogs/ad-law-access/fcc-issues-one-year-limited-waiver-of-new-tcpa-consent-revocation-rule)), keep copy informational, and send only inside 8 am to 8 pm recipient time. Whether oral consent suffices for an on-request informational text was not sourced; counsel should confirm. Start registration now because approval is the long pole, and ship the context packet and fallback callback immediately since they need no registration. Email is a no-registration alternative, but capturing an email by voice is itself a high-risk slot. Effort is M for the packet and callback, L end to end with registration.

## Conclusion

The research reorders the priorities you started with. The noise plan assumes a loudness problem, but your calls show a finalization problem, and the one component of that plan with a platform gap (inbound DTMF on Twilio native) is the least ready to ship. The real exposure is a measurement blind spot: until you have audio-layer evals and per-call slot metrics, every change, including the ones above, is judged by calls you happen to place.

Two uncertainties should shape how you hold this ranking. First, the evidence is almost entirely vendor documentation, so ranks 1, 4 and 5 are well-reasoned bets, not demonstrated effects; ranks 1 and 2 are cheap enough to resolve with your own data. Second, nothing here authorizes real-consumer use. Producer licensing, PA and MI election forms, FCRA, and messaging registration are gates that sit outside the product roadmap, and counsel should clear the consent and licensing items before the line serves anyone beyond the demo.
